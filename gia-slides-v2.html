GIA — Project Category Definition
What It Means
GIA is a category of infrastructure project defined by its scale, ambition and lasting civic or cultural impact. GIA projects are not merely functional — they are intended to become defining landmarks of their city, nation or era.
Examples
Iconic Transit Stations Waterfront Masterplans New City Quarters Transformational Corridors Monumental Public Buildings
4
Qualities
Criteria
What Makes a GIA Project
Four Defining Characteristics
01 — Capital Investment
Significant capital value — minimum $50M — sufficient to warrant the scale and complexity of GIA-category delivery.
02 — Architectural Ambition
Projects intended to become defining landmarks — not merely functional infrastructure but civic and cultural assets of lasting significance.
03 — Complex Multi-Stakeholder Delivery
Specialist advisory capability required across planning, engineering and design. Not routine construction delivery.
04 — Lasting Effect on the Built Environment
Once-in-a-generation opportunities that reshape how people live, move and work in cities and regions.

07 — PROJECT STAGES
ResultsMapSourcesSectorsDefinitions
Project Stage — Procurement Lifecycle
Pipeline
The project has been publicly announced or included in a government investment plan, but formal procurement has not yet commenced.
Recommended Action
Early positioning, teaming discussions and relationship-building with the client body. Begin sector and client intelligence gathering. Respond to any published market engagement exercises.
Procurement
Actively in a pre-tender or tender preparation phase. Pre-Qualification Questionnaires (PQQs), Expressions of Interest (EOIs), or Invitations to Tender (ITTs) have been issued or are imminent.
Recommended Action
Active monitoring — respond to PQQ/EOI, finalise teaming, prepare capability statement and begin win strategy development. This is the recommended action window.
Live Tender
A formal tender is currently open for submission. The opportunity is time-critical — a deadline is active and bid preparation should begin immediately if GIA has not already engaged.
Recommended Action
Begin bid preparation immediately. Confirm team, review ITT documents, register intent and assign BD resource. A pre-existing client relationship is typically needed to compete effectively.
Awarded
The contract has been publicly awarded. Included for market intelligence — not an active bid opportunity for GIA.
Intelligence Value
Understand competitor wins, typical award values, client preferences and programme durations. May indicate follow-on packages, variations or repeat procurements in the near term.

09 — GIA FIT ASSESSMENT
ResultsMapSourcesSectorsDefinitions
GIA Fit Assessment — Four Scoring Dimensions
Sector Fit 0 – 100
How well the opportunity aligns with GIA's 7 sector specialisms. A score of 100 means the opportunity sits squarely in GIA's core offering. Low scores indicate peripheral adjacencies or out-of-scope sectors.
Scale Fit 0 – 100
Whether the project scale (value, complexity, duration) is appropriate for GIA's capacity and track record. Very small packages and very large programmes where GIA would be a sub-tier advisor score lower.
Geography Fit 0 – 100
GIA's existing presence, relationships and market knowledge in the project's region. Established markets (UK, Europe, GCC, Southeast Asia) score higher than frontier markets where GIA has limited prior engagement.
Win Probability 0 – 100
The AI's estimate of likelihood given sector fit, geography, competition level, procurement type and stage. Indicative only — does not account for incumbent advantages, specific client relationships, or internal resource availability.
Assessment Verdict
One-sentence qualitative recommendation synthesising the four scores
Verdict options: Pursue actively — strong alignment across all four dimensions. / Monitor — sector and scale fit strong but geography is a constraint. / Deprioritise — peripheral sector alignment or insufficient scale for GIA engagement.
