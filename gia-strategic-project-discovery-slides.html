02 What is GIA? Global Iconic Architecture — Definition
Global Iconic Architecture
Defining the Category
GIA — Global Iconic Architecture — is a category of infrastructure project defined by its scale, ambition and lasting civic or cultural impact.
Examples
Iconic Transit Stations Waterfront Masterplans New City Quarters Transformational Corridors Monumental Public Buildings
What Makes a GIA Project
Four Defining Qualities
01 — Capital Investment
Significant capital value, typically $50M minimum — sufficient to warrant the scale and complexity of GIA-category delivery.
02 — Architectural Ambition
Projects intended to become defining landmarks — not merely functional infrastructure but civic and cultural assets.
03 — Complex Delivery
Multi-stakeholder programmes requiring specialist advisory capability across planning, engineering and design.
04 — Lasting Effect
Once-in-a-generation opportunities that reshape how people live, move and work in cities and regions.
Why This Tool Exists

07 Project Stages Procurement Lifecycle
Pipeline
Pipeline
The project has been publicly announced or included in a government investment plan, but formal procurement has not yet commenced.
Recommended Action
Early positioning, teaming discussions and relationship-building with the client body.
Procurement
Procurement
Actively in a pre-tender or tender preparation phase. Pre-Qualification Questionnaires (PQQs), Expressions of Interest (EOIs), or Invitations to Tender (ITTs) have been issued or are imminent.
Recommended Action
Active monitoring — respond to PQQ/EOI and initiate pursuit strategy.
Live Tender
Live Tender
A formal tender is currently open for submission. The opportunity is time-critical — a deadline is active and bid preparation should begin immediately if GIA has not already engaged.
Recommended Action
Begin bid preparation immediately. Confirm team, review ITT documents and register interest.
Awarded
Awarded
The contract has been publicly awarded. Included for market intelligence — not an active bid opportunity.
Intelligence Value
Understand competitor wins, typical award values, client preferences and follow-on package opportunities.

09 GIA Fit Assessment Four-Dimension Scoring Model
Sector Fit
0 – 100
How well the opportunity aligns with GIA's 7 sector specialisms. A score of 100 means the opportunity sits squarely in GIA's core offering. Low scores indicate peripheral adjacencies or out-of-scope sectors.
Scale Fit
0 – 100
Whether the project scale (value, complexity, duration) is appropriate for GIA's capacity and track record. Very small packages and programmes where GIA would be a sub-tier advisor score lower.
Geography Fit
0 – 100
GIA's existing presence, relationships and market knowledge in the project's region. Established markets (UK, Europe, GCC, Southeast Asia) score higher than frontier markets where GIA has limited prior engagement.
Win Probability
0 – 100
The AI's estimate of likelihood given sector fit, geography, competition level, procurement type and stage. Indicative only — does not account for incumbent advantages, specific client relationships, or internal resource availability.

Region Filter
Constrains the AI's search to a specific geography. Set to Global by default. Narrowing the region improves result precision but may reduce the diversity of opportunities returned. The filter applies to both single-sector and full scans.

Source Filtering
The Sources tab lets you enable or disable individual procurement databases and publications from the 650+ source library. Enabled sources are listed explicitly in the AI prompt, instructing it to prioritise those channels. Disabling sources reduces — but does not guarantee exclusion of — results from other channels.
